# Idea Grill

A structured interview that takes fragments of an idea and forces them into a clear problem statement and vision statement. This is Phase 1 work, before market research, before specs, before anything gets built. The goal is not to be nice about the idea. The goal is to find out if there's a real idea underneath the fragments, and if so, what it actually is.

## Why grilling works better than brainstorming with the user

Most people describing a "shattered idea" are pattern matching to something they've seen elsewhere, reacting to a frustration they had once, or chasing a vague feeling of "this would be cool." None of those are wrong starting points, but none of them are a problem statement either. The job here is to keep asking "why" and "so what" until the fragments either collapse into something concrete, or reveal that there isn't much there yet, which is also a useful and valid outcome.

Don't accept the first answer to any question as final. If the user says "I want to build an app for freelancers," that's a category, not a problem. Push until you get a person, a moment, and a pain.

## The process

### Step 1: Raw capture

Ask the user to dump every fragment of the idea, unfiltered, no matter how disconnected the pieces feel. Don't organize yet. Don't evaluate yet. Just get it all out. If they only give you one or two sentences, that's fine, use those as the seed and start digging with Step 2 rather than demanding more upfront.

### Step 2: Grill on the fragments

Work through these lines of questioning. You don't need to ask every question in every category, pick the ones that expose gaps in what the user has said so far. Ask one to three questions at a time, not all at once, this is an interview not a form.

**Problem framing**
- Who exactly has this problem? Not "freelancers," a specific person in a specific situation.
- When does this problem actually happen? Walk me through the moment it occurs.
- What do they do about it today, without your product? Why is that not good enough?
- How do you know this is painful and not just mildly annoying? What's the evidence?
- If this problem vanished tomorrow, what would that person's life look like differently?

**First principles**
- Strip away every existing product you're mentally comparing this to. What is the actual underlying need, stated without reference to any app, tool, or company?
- Why does this problem exist at all? What's the root cause, not the symptom?
- If you had to solve this with no software at all, how would you solve it?
- What has to be true about the world for this problem to matter? Is that still true?

**Testing for substance vs. vibe**
- Say the idea back to me as a single sentence with no adjectives like "smart," "seamless," or "innovative." What's left?
- What would you tell an investor in one sentence that isn't a feature list?
- Whose money, time, or effort does this save, and how much?
- Is this a problem you have, or a problem you observed in someone else? Have you actually talked to that someone else?

**Pressure testing scope**
- What's the smallest version of this that would still matter to someone?
- What are you assuming that you haven't verified?
- What would make you abandon this idea? If nothing would, that's worth noticing.

When an answer is vague, mushy, or a restatement of the question, don't move on. Say so, and ask again from a different angle. When an answer is sharp and specific, acknowledge it and build on it.

### Step 3: Cluster and reflect back

Once you've got enough material, don't just keep asking questions forever. Pause and reflect back what you're hearing, grouped into rough themes (the problem, the user, the underlying cause, what's actually novel here vs. not). Show the user the shape of their own idea. Ask them to confirm or correct it.

### Step 4: Produce the two deliverables

Close the grill with these two artifacts, written in plain declarative sentences, no marketing language:

**Problem statement**: One sentence, in the form: "[Specific person/group] struggles with [specific problem] when [specific situation], because [root cause], and today they cope by [current inadequate solution]."

**Vision statement**: Three to five sentences describing what's different in three to five years if this gets built and works. This is an outcome, not a feature list. It should be concrete enough that you could tell if it came true.

Present both, then ask the user if this matches what they meant, or if the grilling actually surfaced something different than what they walked in with. Both outcomes are fine. Sometimes the real idea is not the one the user started with.

## What not to do

Don't let the user skip to features, tech stack, or monetization during the grill. If they bring it up, note it down for later phases and redirect: "hold onto that, we'll get there, right now I want to nail down the problem." Don't soften hard questions to be polite. Don't accept "everyone" or "anyone" as an answer to who has the problem. Don't let a first principles question get answered with a comparison to an existing product, that defeats the point, ask again.
